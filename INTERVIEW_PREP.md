## The one-minute answer (say the problem first, no screen)

A front-line sales hire goes through several interviews, each with a different form. The forms do not test what the job now demands. Months later the hire leaves and the company pays again. We give the hiring manager evidence, not a verdict: six capability areas, scored by plain rules, each result traceable to the questions behind it, in three languages. The manager still decides.

## Likely questions and honest answers

1. **How do you know a score predicts success?** We do not claim it. With about 10 employees we show a range, not a correlation. We record every hire's outcome so the evidence grows. Prediction needs more people followed for longer than 10 weeks.
2. **Who on your team knows psychometrics?** Nobody does, and we say so. We ask for an HR lead and, if available, an industrial and organisational psychologist to review the job analysis and item keys. Fallback: a published competency framework, and the report states keying was not psychologist-reviewed.
3. **How do you avoid bias?** The role profile comes from a job analysis, not from our opinion. No AI scoring, so every score is explainable. Item difficulty is compared across languages. Optional, consented, separate group monitoring. No automated rejection.
4. **Are the three languages really equivalent?** Independent translation, back-translation, one item ID and one key, then a cross-language difficulty check on pilot data. Until that runs, results are provisional.
5. **What if you cannot get 20 to 30 candidates or 10 employees?** We use volunteers and declared synthetic data, say so in the report, and deliver the mechanism with its limits stated.
6. **Why is the pilot over year-end?** It is the weak point in our plan. We propose employee sessions in week 7 and the candidate pilot from week 8 into week 10.
7. **Candidates can share answers or get coached.** Question pools, server-side timer, forced-choice self-report, and the profile lists two or three items to re-ask live.
8. **Privacy?** Consent text first, retention set by HR, export and delete on request, employee identities pseudonymised with the key held by HR, audit log on every view.
9. **What can you show today?** No code. Say it plainly. If there are three or more days before the interview, show a thin working slice, see below.
10. **How much time can you give?** Answer with real numbers. Disclose other commitments, including the Codex hackathon on 5 Dec. Do not claim full-time if it is not true.
11. **Why this stack?** React, Tailwind, Node.js, Express, MySQL: the backend lead already works in it, so nobody learns a new framework.
12. **Why not buy an existing tool such as Mercer Mettl?** Fair question, and the category is not new. We found Mandarin support but no confirmed Bahasa Malaysia sales test. Our difference is the role profile the company agrees, flags that separate a trainable gap from a risk, a question bank the company owns, and validation limits stated honestly. We have not compared pricing, so we quote none. See MARKET_SCAN.md.

## Questions to ask them

- Who signs off the role profile, and can managers join a 2-hour workshop in week 1?
- Is access to about 10 employees and their performance data committed, and who consents them?
- How many real candidates a week between weeks 8 and 10?
- Where can data be hosted, and what is the retention rule?
- Year-end shutdown dates?
